Stage 4 — Aim the sampling¶

Purpose¶

Give every regime enough draws to support an index. On the default ranges one regime holds almost the whole sample, so the others are not under-resolved — they are absent. This stage measures how often each preset actually hits its regime, then sizes and runs a scan per regime from that measurement.

Important

This is the stage that creates the study. Without it there is one regime and no comparison to make.

At a glance¶

Inputs

REGIME_PRESETS / REGIME_PRESETS_L5, target_cluster=1500, probe_n=250, safety=1.15, n_max=200000

Outputs

{regime: n_draws}, then scan_<regime>.csv per regime

Code

size_draws_for_target, run_targeted_regime_scans, load_regime_scans

Cost

A probe of 250 draws per regime — about 15% — then the real scans

Concepts¶

A preset is a set of range overrides that pushes the sampler towards one regime. A preset’s yield is the fraction of its draws that land in the regime it targets. The draw count you want is target / yield, but the yield is only knowable by running the preset — which is the circularity this stage breaks.

Procedure¶

  1. For each regime, run a cheap probe of probe_n=250 draws under that regime’s preset. The probe is its own LHS and is discarded.

  2. Measure the yield: the fraction of probe draws whose label matches the target regime.

  3. Refuse with an explanation if the yield is zero — a preset that never hits its regime cannot be sized, only fixed.

  4. Size the real run as target_cluster / yield × safety, with safety = 1.15.

  5. Refuse to size a run above n_max = 200000, and say why.

  6. Run one clean scan per regime at the sized draw count, saving scan_<regime>.csv.

  7. load_regime_scans reloads them, checking the study stamp.

Design choices¶

Why a probe rather than a cached yield. The yields used to be measured once by hand and pasted into the configuration as MEASURED_YIELDS_*. That cache went stale silently: edit any range or preset and the numbers still looked authoritative while the clusters came out unbalanced — which makes a cross-regime comparison partly a comparison of estimator noise rather than of sensitivity. The probe costs about 15% and is correct for whatever study, material, temperature and preset are active.

Why safety = 1.15. The probe yield is a binomial estimate. At the hardest preset observed — surface, with yield around 0.23 — a 250-draw probe has a standard error near 0.027, about 12% relative. Without a margin the cluster lands short roughly half the time.

Why n_max refuses rather than warns. A preset targeting an unreachable regime otherwise asks for a silently impossible scan, and the run simply never finishes. Refusing names the preset.

Why one clean design rather than concatenated batches. Topping up a short cluster by appending another batch produces a sample that is not a Latin hypercube over anything, which undermines the space-filling property the estimators rely on. The probe is discarded and the real scan is run once, correctly sized.

Checks and failure modes¶

Symptom

Meaning

ValueError: yield is zero

The preset never hits its regime. Fix the preset; no draw count will help.

Refused: above n_max

The regime is effectively unreachable on these ranges.

A cluster lands short anyway

Probe noise. Raise probe_n or safety.

A preset that pins nothing

Caught at stage 1, and now fails loudly. It would otherwise produce an unaimed scan wearing a regime’s name.

A parameter pinned by the preset ranks below the floor

Expected, and not a statement about its physical leverage — Figure 20.

Clusters balanced but indices still noisy

target_cluster may be too small for the parameter count; see the high-dimensionality note in Part IV — The sensitivity workflow.
