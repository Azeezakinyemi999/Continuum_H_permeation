C.2 Study stamp — study.txt¶

One line recording which study produced the scans in that folder. Its purpose is to make a mismatched reload fail loudly instead of silently analysing another material system’s samples.

C.3 Comparison matrices¶

compare_delta_flux.csv and siblings: one row per parameter, one column per regime, holding the index for that metric. This is what the regime comparison heatmap and the parallel-coordinates plot read.

C.4 Figures¶

Each notebook writes into <Notebook>-results/ beside itself — a PNG at 450 dpi and a PDF of the same stem. The folder is resolved against the working directory, which for a correctly launched notebook is its own study folder.

Pressure exponents convert the same way only when the exponent is the same. A permeability in mol m⁻¹ s⁻¹ Pa⁻⁰·⁵ and one in mol m⁻¹ s⁻¹ Pa⁻¹ are not related by a unit factor; see Figure 5.

Important

Whether a quoted flux counts moles of H or moles of H₂ changes it by a factor of two, and that convention is not recorded in the configuration. The model is internally consistent — \(K_s\), \(C\) and \(J\) all use the same species — so every ratio, fraction and PRF in this documentation is unaffected. Fix the convention against your own source data before comparing an absolute flux with an experiment.

E. Checking the equations¶

docs/figures/check_equations.py restates every equation in Part II independently, in plain arithmetic on the Example Wall, and compares it with what calculations returns. A check that merely called the same function twice would pass forever and prove nothing, so no expected value is borrowed from the module under test.

Run it from the repository root:

python docs/figures/check_equations.py

E.1 What it checks¶

Group

Checks

sorption and Fick

Sieverts \(C=K_s\sqrt{P}\), \(C(0)=0\), Henry \(C=K_{ox}P\), Fick

Level 1

flux, \(\Phi=D_mK_s\), \(C_{up}\), and the \(\sqrt{P}\) signature

Level 2a

flux, \(R_{ox}\), linearity in \(P\), and \(R_{metal}\)

Level 2b

\(P_{int}\) against the closed-form quadratic root, flux, flux balance, and that the series flux is below both single-layer fluxes

Level 2b limit

\(L_{ox}\to0\) recovers Level 1

Level 3

the area-weighted sum, and that contributions sum to the total

Level 3 limit

\(f_d\to0\) recovers Level 2b

Level 4

\(K=\exp(E_b/RT)\), the trapping term, \(D_{eff}\), and that trapping retards

Level 4 limit

an empty trap list recovers \(D_{lattice}\) exactly

Arrhenius

the reference-temperature form, and \(k(T_{ref})=k_{ref}\)

Level 6

\(g(\theta)\), the closed-form \(\sqrt{P_{int}}\), the rate law, flux balance at the solution, \(0<\theta<1\), and that a finite surface rate costs flux

permeability tiers

tier-1 values, the units each model assigns, that Model 1 refuses to combine, and the Model 2 series value

pressure exponent

\(n=\tfrac12\) for Sieverts, \(n=1\) for Henry, and strictly between for the mixed wall

documentation

the vendored palette matches model_config.COLORS

Where no independent form exists — the Level 2b and Level 6 solves — the check is an invariant instead: flux balance must hold at the solution, and a limit must collapse to the level below.

E.2 Expected output¶

The last line is the one to read:

44 / 44 checks passed

Exit code is 0 when every check passes and 1 otherwise. A FAIL line names the check, what was computed, what the documented equation gives, and the relative difference.

Note

Two tolerances are used. Equations that should agree to machine precision are held to 1e-9; anything that went through a root finder is held to 1e-6. The Level 2b closed-form check currently agrees to about 2e-16.

F. Glossary¶

Apparent permeability — a permeability backed out of a solved flux. Tier 4: a property of the wall and the experiment, so it is always reported with its operating point.

Dummy parameter — a parameter the model never reads, carried through the whole sensitivity analysis so that whatever index it earns defines the noise floor.

Example Wall — the small fictitious system in docs/figures/example_wall.py that every figure and equation check runs on. Not a real material.

Henry’s law — concentration linear in pressure, \(C=K_{ox}P\). Applies when molecular H₂ dissolves intact, which in this model is the oxide under Model 1.

Intact path — the part of the wall area where the oxide is unbroken, as opposed to the defect path. The two are in parallel.

Model 1 — dissociation at the oxide/metal interface. Henry oxide, Sieverts metal. Levels 1–5.

Model 2 — dissociation at the gas/oxide surface. Sieverts oxide, Sieverts metal. The L6 family.

Oriani model — local-equilibrium trapping, in which trapped and lattice hydrogen are in equilibrium at every point. Valid while trap occupancy stays low; the alternative when it does not is McNabb–Foster.

PAWN — a density-based global sensitivity index, computed from the distance between conditional and unconditional output distributions. Needs no variance decomposition.

Permeance — a permeability divided by a thickness: what a specific wall passes, rather than what the material passes.

PRF — permeation reduction factor. How much less flux the coated wall passes than the bare one. PRF = 1 means the coating does nothing.

Pressure exponent \(n\) — \(\mathrm{d}\ln J/\mathrm{d}\ln P\). A model-free diagnostic of which step controls the wall: \(\tfrac12\) for a Sieverts step, \(1\) for a Henry layer.

Regime — which physical process carries most of the resistance. Assigned from the resistance fractions, and the basis on which the sensitivity analysis is stratified.

Sieverts’ law — concentration in the square root of pressure, \(C=K_s\sqrt{P}\). Applies when H₂ dissociates before dissolving, which is always true of the metal.

Study — a complete physical system: a metal, an oxide, a microstructure, a defect set and an operating point, living in one study_config.py inside its own folder.

δ (delta) — Borgonovo’s moment-independent importance measure. Like PAWN, it needs no variance decomposition, which is why both survive here.
